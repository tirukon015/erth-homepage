## What’s the Difference Between E-Waste Disposal and Recycling?
Getting rid of an old electronic device and recycling it are not quite the same thing. Here’s the difference.
Electronic Disposal
E-waste disposal means throwing unwanted electronics away, usually with general rubbish or in places where they can eventually end up in landfill. This gets the device out of your home or office, but valuable materials inside it are lost, and harmful substances can end up affecting the soil, water or environment.
E-Waste Recycling
E-waste recycling gives unwanted electronics a different route. Devices are collected, sorted and taken apart so useful materials such as metals and plastics can be recovered and used again. Parts that need special handling can also be separated instead of sending the entire device to landfill.
The Simple Difference: Disposal gets rid of the device. Recycling recovers what can still be used from it.

## Everything to Know Before Pickup
Is ERTH e-waste pickup free?
Free pickup is available when your order includes at least 3 listed devices. If you have fewer than 3 listed devices, the pickup fee is RM50. For example, if you have 3 listed phones, laptops or other listed electronics in one collection request, you qualify for free pickup.
Can I drop my electronics off myself?
Yes. ERTH has a 24/7 drop-off point in Cyberjaya, so you can bring your electronics yourself instead of booking a collection. ERTH also has public collection boxes available at selected locations.
Do you collect large electronics too?
Yes, but some bulky electronics have an additional collection fee because they require more handling. Items such as CRT devices, air conditioners and TVs larger than 50 inches are charged RM50 per unit for pickup.
How much can I get paid for working electronics?
Working devices can earn RM10–500, depending on the device, specifications and condition. Laptops and CPUs range from RM15–350, while Gaming, i7 and Apple 2015+ devices can reach RM500. Working monitors, projectors, TVs and smartphones range from RM10–25. Working devices must be tested by ERTH or supported with video proof. T&Cs apply.
Can I still get paid for non-working or scrap electronics?
Yes. Non-working devices can still have recycling value. ERTH offers up to RM15 for servers, RM10 for laptops, CPUs and all-in-one computers, RM5 for selected phones, tablets, TVs, monitors, projectors and cartridges, and RM2 for printers. Cables and wires are valued at RM1/kg, while mixed e-waste is RM0.50/kg. T&Cs apply.
How do you decide whether my device counts as working?
ERTH can test the device directly, or you may be asked to provide video proof through WhatsApp showing that it works. Under the current cash-reward terms, a device is considered “working” when it is less than 5 years old and meets the required working condition.
How will I receive my cash reward?
Cash rewards are paid cashlessly, so you do not need to collect physical cash. Payment options listed by ERTH include bank transfer, GrabPay, Touch ’n Go eWallet, BigPay and Boost.
Can stairs affect how much I get paid?
Yes. The standard cash reward assumes that ERTH can collect the electronics from the ground floor or from a floor accessible by lift, so If your building has a working lift, this stair reduction does not apply.
If the collection team has to carry items down stairs, the reward may be reduced:
1 floor by stairs: 25% reduction
2 floors: 50% reduction
3 floors: 75% reduction
4 floors: 100% reduction, meaning no cash reward
Can ERTH cancel my pickup?
Yes. ERTH's terms state that a pickup may be cancelled when necessary, including when a collection cannot be fulfilled by an available Hero. If you have an important deadline, such as moving or clearing a property, share it when you make your request.
Can I ask for my electronics back after ERTH collects them?
If you need an item returned after collection, a return transport fee plus RM250 per item will apply. It’s best to double-check that you’re ready to part with the device before pickup.

## 1. Key Customer Groups and Their Priorities
The homepage has to serve several customer types, but they do not all deserve equal weight. The primary journey should be the person or business that has electronics they need removed and wants to know: Can you take this, will you come to me, what do I need to do, and can I trust you with it?
A. Households with old or unwanted electronics
This is probably the widest and simplest customer group.
They may have:
An old laptop sitting in a drawer
A broken TV
Phones they stopped using years ago
A monitor taking up space
An appliance they do not know how to dispose of
Multiple old devices accumulated over time
Electronics left behind after moving, renovating or replacing equipment
Their biggest priority is getting rid of the item easily.
They are generally not waking up thinking:
“I need to participate in responsible e-waste management.”
They are thinking:
“I need this thing out of my house.”
Then secondary questions appear:
Do you take broken devices?
Do I need to bring it somewhere?
Will you collect just one item?
Does pickup cost anything?
Can I get paid for this?
What happens to my data?
Are you available where I live?
Is this legitimate?
So the homepage needs to meet them at the disposal problem first, then connect that convenience to responsible recycling.
Main promise for this group:You have electronics you no longer need. We make getting rid of them easy.
B. People with broken, damaged or non-working electronics
This group deserves explicit attention because their uncertainty is different.
A customer with a perfectly functional laptop may assume somebody wants it.
A customer with:
a cracked phone
a dead laptop
a television that no longer turns on
missing chargers
damaged components
very old equipment
often assumes:
“They probably won't take this.”
That uncertainty can stop booking before it begins.
The homepage therefore needs to communicate very early that the service is not only for beautiful, working electronics.
Language such as:
Old, broken, damaged or simply no longer needed
is much more useful than repeatedly saying only:
Recycle your e-waste.
This group needs particularly clear device-condition information.
The homepage should answer:
Working?
Non-working?
Damaged?
Missing accessories?
Beyond repair?
Very old?
Unsure what it is worth?
They should not have to contact ERTH just to discover whether a broken laptop counts as e-waste.
C. Convenience-first customers
This is technically a behaviour rather than a demographic, but it is one of the most important customer groups strategically.
These people may already know that recycling is the correct thing to do.
Their barrier is:
effort.
They do not want to:
Search for a recycling centre
Figure out opening hours
Put a large TV into their car
Drive somewhere
Carry equipment
Sort through complicated recycling rules
Contact several vendors
Spend their weekend dealing with old electronics
For them, doorstep pickup is the product.
This is why the homepage should never bury pickup under environmental messaging.
Compare:
Recycle your electronics responsibly with ERTH.
with:
We collect your unwanted electronics from your doorstep and make sure they are handled responsibly.
The second one removes the customer's biggest objection immediately.
The emotional equation is:
I want this gone + I don't want the hassle = book pickup.
D. Customers hoping to receive value for their electronics
There is also a financially motivated group.
They are thinking:
“If this still has value, can I get something for it?”
Payment can be a strong booking incentive, but it needs to be handled carefully because not every piece of e-waste necessarily has resale or recovery value.
So money should be a supporting conversion driver, not the universal headline promise.
The homepage needs to distinguish between:
Electronics ERTH can collect
Electronics that may qualify for payment
Devices that can still be responsibly recycled but have little/no monetary value
Otherwise customers can interpret:
Get paid for your e-waste
as:
ERTH pays for every electronic item.
Then an attractive hook creates disappointment later.
Better messaging makes value conditional:
Some eligible devices may even earn you cash.
The dominant reason to book remains convenient disposal. Payment makes an already useful service more attractive.
E. Customers worried about personal data
This is especially important for:
Laptops
Desktop computers
Phones
Hard drives
Tablets
Storage devices
Their physical disposal problem is attached to another worry:

Great service copy often feels effortless because it eliminates one uncertainty after another.
Priority 5: Avoid making the customer sound responsible for the process
One of the previous-page problems was that the wording made ERTH's convenient service sound like work for the customer.
That needs to disappear.
Instead of a customer-centred process like:

the language should make ERTH the active party:

where operationally accurate.

It sounds worthy but doesn't tell customers what happens.
Something like this has more meaning:
Your old electronics stay out of landfill and are directed toward reuse or responsible recycling where appropriate.
What actually happens is more convincing than generic environmental virtue.
Priority 7: Be very careful with “free” and “cash”
Both are high-attention words.
Therefore qualification should be close enough that people cannot misunderstand them.
Don't imply:

unless that is actually the policy.
Don't imply:

Instead clearly explain which service, quantity, location or device requirements affect eligibility.
Trust is more valuable than a misleading high-click headline.
